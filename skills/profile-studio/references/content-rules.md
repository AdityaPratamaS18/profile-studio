# Content rules

## Plan before design

A profile fails on content more often than on design. Agree the words first, in the Word review copy.
Design changes after sign-off are cheap. Content changes after design cost a full rebuild and review.

## Sources

1. Collect every source: the old profile, the website, brochures, the brief, the client's own notes.
2. Make a list of facts: year started, team size, offices, services, clients, people and titles.
3. When two sources disagree, ask the user which source is final (often the live website). Then use it everywhere.
4. Never invent a client, a quote, a number or a qualification. Leave the field out instead.

## Page order that works

1. Cover
2. Contents
3. About (what the firm does, in one sentence, then proof)
4. Leadership
5. Mission, vision and values
6. Clients and testimonials (early: proof sells before the service list)
7. Services overview
8. Why choose us
9. One page for each service line
10. Team
11. Contact

Group the services into 4 to 7 lines the client uses in its own sales talk. Ask the client for the lines
if the old material lists 20 loose services.

## Writing

- Write for the buyer, not for the firm: what the buyer gets, not what the firm is proud of.
- One idea in each sentence. Short words. No buzzwords such as "synergy", "world-class", "one-stop".
- Use the buyer's names for things. Use numbers when they are true and checkable.
- Keep every service page to its fixed shape: intro, 3 to 5 blocks, "who it is for".
- Testimonials: real words from real clients, with a role and a company. Get permission to print them.
- Write in the client's language and spelling. Check names, titles and phone numbers twice.

## Review copy

`PROFILE docx` makes a clean document for the client: a short note on what the document is, a page plan,
then the words for each page. It has no design notes. Send it, collect the changes, edit `profile.json`,
raise `version`, and send it again until the client signs off.
